Anatomy

How A 170 MPH
Building Goes Together.

Scroll. Your building assembles one layer at a time, from the anchors up.

01Foundation
  1. 01 · Foundation

    Anchored at every post.

    On concrete: a 4" slab at 2,500 psi with a thickened 12" × 12" edge and two #5 rebar running continuous. A ½" × 5½" wedge anchor goes within 6" of every side-wall post.

    No slab? 30" helical earth augers bolt through the base rail at each post.

    4" slab · 2,500 psi12" × 12" edge(2) #5 rebar
  2. 02 · Frame

    Steel tube, 4' on center.

    Frames repeat down the full length of your building: 5' apart on Standard builds, 4' apart on High-Wind. 14-gauge tube is standard. Wide spans in high-wind counties step up to 12-gauge.

    The truss gets heavier as your building gets wider. Find your frame ↓

    14-ga standard12-ga high-wind4' or 5' o.c.
  3. 03 · Bracing

    Knee braces lock the corners.

    A knee brace ties each post to its rafter at 45° to 60°. It's 24" minimum on posts up to 7' and 48" minimum on taller posts. That stiff corner is what keeps the frame square when the wind loads the wall.

    45°–60°24" / 48" min
  4. 04 · Skin

    Panels fastened to every frame.

    Ribbed galvanized steel panels screw to the frame and girts. Corner, rake and eave trim close every edge, and a ridge cap seals the peak.

    Galvanized steelFull trim package
  5. 05 · Openings

    Doors framed like structure.

    Every roll-up door gets a 12-gauge post on each side. Side-wall roll-ups up to 16' wide get a structural header. In coastal counties, your doors and windows are Hi-Impact rated.

    12-ga door postsStructural headerHi-Impact on the coast

    FAQ

    Straight
    Answers.

    Still have a question? Call 561-771-5555 and talk to someone who builds these.

    What does "hurricane-rated" actually mean?

    Your building is engineered and certified to a design wind speed: 150 mph on our Standard frame, 170 mph on High-Wind. The engineering drawings are stamped for your county, and that's what your permit office reviews.

    Is a 170 mph building rated for a Category 5?

    The two scales don't line up one-to-one. Building codes rate wind as a 3-second gust, while the hurricane category scale uses 1-minute sustained wind, which reads lower for the same storm. The number that matters is your county's code design wind speed, and your building's rating has to meet or beat it.

    What wind rating do I need?

    It depends on your exact location. West Palm Beach is 169 mph, Stuart 162, Tampa 141 and Orlando 137 (ASCE 7-22, Risk Category II). Use the city check at the top of this page. If your code number is over 150, you need High-Wind framing.

    Do I need engineered plans for a permit?

    In hurricane-prone Florida counties, yes. Every StormSafe building comes with engineered drawings for your county's wind zone, ready for permitting.

    Do I need impact-rated doors and windows?

    In coastal impact counties, yes. We spec Hi-Impact rated windows and walk doors there automatically, so your build passes the first time.

    Do I need a concrete slab?

    Concrete gets wedge anchors at every post. Without a slab, 30" helical earth augers bolt through the base rail. Your drawings call out the anchoring for your site.

    Can I customize a hurricane-rated building?

    Yes. Size, doors, windows, colors and insulation are all yours to pick. The rating comes from the frame and anchoring, so you can configure freely and the engineering follows.
